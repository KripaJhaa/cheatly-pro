How Cheatly works,
in plain English.

A practical introduction to private terminal overlays, screen sharing, AI tools, and responsible live work.

What is Cheatly?

Imagine you are on a video call and you need two things at the same time: the screen everyone else is looking at, and a small desk beside it where you can keep notes, commands, and approved AI help. Cheatly is that second desk for your Mac. It is a private PTY-powered terminal overlay designed for screen sharing.

When you open Cheatly, you get a real terminal window. You can use command-line tools already installed on your Mac, such as git, scripts, Codex, Claude, local LLM tools, and other developer utilities.

How does the private view work?

Cheatly uses a separate window layer and macOS screen-capture behavior. You decide which application or screen to share in your meeting app. The audience sees the call, presentation, browser, or document you selected, while your Cheatly terminal remains in your own workspace.

Capture behavior can vary between Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, WhatsApp, and other apps. Always test the exact setup before an important call.

Can it help during an interview?

Cheatly can help with interview preparation before the call: practise a system-design question, check your code, ask an LLM to explain a difficult idea, or keep a short checklist nearby. During a live interview, use AI tools only when the interviewer or company rules clearly allow them. Cheatly is a workspace tool, not a way to secretly get answers or misrepresent your skills.

Why use a private terminal for live work?

A private terminal gives you one predictable place for notes, commands, research, and preparation. Use ⌘ N to show or hide the overlay and ⌘ M to let clicks pass through to the window underneath. Copy and paste work like a normal terminal.

Share what you intend to share, keep approved supporting tools close, and stay present with the people in the call.
